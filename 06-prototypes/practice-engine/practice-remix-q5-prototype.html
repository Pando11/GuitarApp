One weak spot, three framings

A rough take of the practice-remix idea: the same student weak pair, framed three different ways. Pick a framing to see the drill.

Sage: "Your weakest chord pair right now is G to D — fluency 0.25, about 15 clean changes a minute. The diagnostic line is 30 a minute and the goal is 60. Same pair, three ways to practice it. Which one do you want to try?"

The weak pair (from fluency-store memory)
G ↔ D
both chords are taught in the curriculum — G first appears in Lesson 08 (new-chord-g), D in Lesson 10 (new-chord-d). This pair is the lowest-fluency active pair in memory.
Stored fluency: 0.25
Clean changes/min: 15 (0.25 × 60, per fluency-store scoring)
Diagnostic line: 30/min (score 0.5)
Goal: 60/min (score 1.0)
G — middle on 3rd fret low E, index on 2nd fret A, ring on 3rd fret high e (verified shape from shapes.json)
D — index on 2nd fret G, ring on 3rd fret B, middle on 2nd fret high e (verified shape from shapes.json)
Tempo challenge
Drill (reuses the engine's Tempo-Scaled Loop / Metronome ladder)
metronome 15 → 18 → 21 → 24 → 30 BPM-on-the-change, one clean G↔D per click
Start the click at 15 changes/min (your stored rate). Each time you land a clean change on the beat for a full bar, step the click up one notch. The line to beat is 30/min — that's the diagnostic, not a wall. Miss it and Sage just reports the number and feeds memory; the next linear technique lesson is not blocked.
"You're at 15 a minute on this pair. Let's nudge it: 15, then 18, then 21. Stop the instant a change isn't clean — a muddled change doesn't count, per the 30/60 sensor. We're measuring the real number, not racing it."
Play-along groove
Drill (reuses the engine's Weak-Pair Review loop)
loop: G - D - G - D  |  one bar each, steady 4/4 at 15/min
Same G↔D pair, but inside a four-bar loop you can stay on. Hold each chord a full bar and let it ring. This is the framing that maps onto a real song shape — Amazing Grace (SP11, traditional / public domain) runs G - C - G - D - G, so the D landing is the same move you'll use in a tune you already have every chord for.
"Same two chords, but now they're a loop, not a drill. Keep it even — one bar G, one bar D. When it feels like a groove instead of a change, that's the pair sticking."
Calm slow
Drill (slowed loop — same pair, lower tempo)
loop: G - D  |  8 slow counts per chord, ~15/min, no click pressure
The same G↔D pair at the same stored 15/min, but with no metronome pushing. Eight slow counts on G, eight on D, full ring, no rush. This is the framing for a tired hand or a short session — it still records the pair in memory, it just doesn't score against the 30/min line.
"No clock on this one. Eight slow counts on G, eight on D, let them ring. You're still practicing the pair — we're just not racing the number today."
