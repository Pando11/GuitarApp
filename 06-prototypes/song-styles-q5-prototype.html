Why this feels like folk A steady strum with held beats — some notes ring longer, the rhythm breathes. Same four chords, but it lands like something you'd sit around a fire with, not rush through.

Why this feels like blues A swing feel on the strums — the beats lean, not straight. Less notes than the folk one, but the ones that hit carry more weight. The rhythm has a push-pull to it.
"Blues doesn't mean more chords — it means the time feels different. Hit the downbeat harder, let the upstroke be lighter, and don't rush. The same G–D–Em–C can sound completely different just by how the beats land."

Why this feels like punk Short, hard downstrokes on each beat — nothing fancy, nothing held. The silence between the strums is part of it. Driving, not busy.
"Punk is the simplest one to play and the hardest to get right — because there's nowhere to hide. One hard downstroke per beat, let it ring for a split second, then stop. If you rush, it falls apart. If you don't, it's a rock rhythm."
