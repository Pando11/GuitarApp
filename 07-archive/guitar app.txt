What do you think is a reasonable target?

Given the scale we just saw — tens of millions of downloads across 4-5 established players — here's how I'd think about a realistic target for a new entrant, with the caveat that this is a judgment call, not a market-researched forecast.

Rough benchmarks for a mobile education app, first few years

Year 1 (launch + early traction): 10,000–50,000 downloads, with maybe 1,000–5,000 paying subscribers if you're converting at a typical 5-10% freemium rate. This is "prove people want it" territory, not growth-at-scale.
Year 2-3 (product-market fit found): 250,000–1,000,000 downloads, 15,000–75,000 paying subscribers. This is roughly where a differentiated niche player would sit — well below Gibson App's ~1M learner base, but a real, sustainable business.
Year 5 (established player): Low single-digit millions of downloads, 100,000–300,000 paying subscribers, would put you meaningfully below Yousician/Simply Guitar but on par with or ahead of Fender Play's reach.

Why I'd anchor there, not higher

You're not first-mover in "AI listens to guitar" — that ship has sailed. Your wedge is the differentiated features (camera technique coaching, jam sessions, AI backing band), which is a real edge but a niche-within-a-niche, not something that reroutes the whole market to you
Consumer education apps have brutal retention — most of these companies' "millions of users" figures include huge numbers of people who downloaded, tried a free lesson, and left. Realistic subscriber targets should assume you'll lose the large majority of downloads within 30 days, same as everyone else in this space

The more useful question
Rather than a download number, I'd set your first real target as: can you get 500-1,000 genuinely engaged beta users who use the AI feedback + jam sessions weekly and would be upset if you shut it down? That's a far better signal than a download count, and it's achievable in months, not years — it tells you whether the differentiation actually lands before you spend real money on growth.

What 1,000 monthly users actually gets you

At a $10-15/mo subscription price (in line with what we saw from competitors), 1,000 paying users is $10,000-15,000/month, or $120K-180K/year in recurring revenue. That's a real, sustainable small business — not a rounding error.
It's also a far more achievable target than it looks next to "20 million MAU." You don't need viral growth or huge ad spend — a few hundred engaged guitar students who love the jam sessions and tell their friends, plus some organic search/social presence, can realistically get you there.
Small numbers like this also mean you can actually know your users — read every piece of feedback, watch how people really use the jam feature, adjust fast. That's an advantage the big players lose at scale.

One nuance worth deciding now: is 1,000 monthly active users (people who show up and use it) or 1,000 paying subscribers? Those are different bars — active users usually need a bigger download base underneath them (maybe 5,000-10,000 downloads to net 1,000 actives, depending on retention). Worth being clear with yourself on which one you're aiming for, since it changes how much top-of-funnel marketing you actually need.
